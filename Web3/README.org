* Web3 Engineering for Staff and Principal Engineers
Twenty files on building, securing, operating, and leading Web3 systems, from chain fundamentals and the EVM to protocol design, incident response, and technical leadership, plus this guide. Follow links with =C-c C-o= (cursor on the link) and return with =C-c &=.

** Scope and expectations
- *Focus*: engineering judgment for systems where code holds value, state is public, mistakes are often irreversible, and the environment is adversarial. The emphasis is on threat models, trust assumptions, invariants, operations, and decision quality as much as on syntax and tools.
- *Level*: for engineers who can already program and want Staff and Principal depth: owning security, architecture, reliability, and the decisions around them. Each file begins with a Staff-level lens.
- *Primary platform*: Ethereum and EVM chains, including rollups. Other ecosystems appear where they clarify trade-offs.
- *What these notes are not*: investment, legal, or tax advice; a catalog of tokens or projects; a substitute for audits; or a guarantee that any practice makes a system safe.

** State of the ecosystem (as of October 2026)
Protocol and tooling facts change quickly. These were checked against primary sources while writing; re-verify before relying on them.
- *Ethereum upgrades*: Pectra activated in May 2025 (including EIP-7702 account delegation, higher validator maximum balance, BLS12-381 precompiles, a higher calldata floor for data-heavy transactions). Fusaka activated on 3 December 2025 (PeerDAS for data availability sampling, blob-parameter-only forks to raise blob capacity, and other changes such as a per-transaction gas cap). Glamsterdam is the next planned fork (enshrined proposer-builder separation, block-level access lists, gas repricing); its testnet activation was scheduled for October 2026 and a mainnet date should be checked.
- *Solidity*: the 0.8.x line continues; 0.8.36 (July 2026) added support for the Amsterdam EVM version; recent releases default the EVM version to osaka. The repository moved to the argotorg organization on GitHub.
- *Tooling*: Foundry remains the default Solidity-native toolchain; Hardhat 3 is the current Hardhat major version, with Solidity tests alongside TypeScript tests.
- *Layer 2s*: L2BEAT's stages framework classifies rollups by decentralization of their security mechanisms; most rollups are at Stage 0 or 1 and few at Stage 2. Check the current framework and each project's risk summary.

** How to use these files
1. Read in order the first time. Files 01 to 04 give the common vocabulary; the rest can be reordered by your role (see the paths below).
2. Apply each file to a system you know or to a toy protocol you build: write its threat model, invariants, tests, and runbooks.
3. Do the practice items and the capstones on a local node or fork. Never test attacks against live systems or other people's funds.
4. Verify protocol-state claims against primary sources before relying on them (EIPs, client and compiler release notes, L2 risk summaries).
5. Keep an engineering notebook of decisions, trust assumptions, and incident lessons; use the decision-record practices in =18_architecture-decisions.org= and the Software Architecture notes.

** A checklist of Staff-level Web3 judgment
Rate yourself honestly; the items are skills, not facts.
- [ ] I can state a system's trust assumptions, admin powers, dependencies, and exit paths, and I do not overstate decentralization.
- [ ] I write specifications, invariants, and threat models before code, and I test invariants with fuzzing and fork tests.
- [ ] I know the main vulnerability classes, token quirks, signature pitfalls, and oracle and MEV risks, and I design against them.
- [ ] I model the transaction lifecycle, finality, and reorgs, and I build services that are idempotent and reorg-safe.
- [ ] I choose chains, L2s, bridges, and vendors from their real security properties and record the decision with review triggers.
- [ ] I deploy through reproducible, rehearsed, gated pipelines with safe key handling, and I govern upgrades transparently.
- [ ] I plan audits, bounties, monitoring, and incident response as one program and rehearse containment.
- [ ] I communicate risk to executives, lawyers, auditors, and communities in their terms, and I name risk owners.
- [ ] I keep up with protocol and tooling change through primary sources and a disciplined adoption process.
- [ ] I protect people and keys: operational security, sustainable on-call, and honest communication.
