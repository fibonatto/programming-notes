* Software Architecture
Ten files on architectural decision-making for Staff and Principal engineers: styles, boundaries, coupling, interfaces, patterns, quality attributes, decision records, evolution, technical debt, and architecture review. This page gives the reading order, what to do with each file, and links to the sections that matter most. Follow links with =C-c C-o= (cursor on the link) and return with =C-c &=.

** Scope and expectations
- *Focus*: how to decide, document, evolve, and review the structure of software systems, with explicit trade-offs. The emphasis is on judgment: forces, evidence, costs, reversibility, and communication.
- *Level*: for engineers who can build systems and now shape them across teams. The notes assume working familiarity with services, databases, and delivery pipelines.
- *What these notes are not*: a catalog of technologies or cloud services, a methodology to follow blindly, or a substitute for knowing your own system. Architectural advice is contextual; every file states costs and when not to use a technique.
- *Relation to the Foundations notes*: this folder applies the theory in the Foundations folder (complexity, data structures, type systems, memory, concurrency and distributed systems) to design decisions. Useful companions are listed below.

** How to use these files
1. Read in order the first time; later use the files as references by topic.
2. Apply each file to a system you know: draw its real boundaries, write its real decisions, list its real debt. Reading without applying yields little.
3. Do the exercises with a real codebase and real stakeholders where possible. The capstone projects below integrate several files.
4. Treat every table and checklist as a starting point for your context, not as a rule.
5. Read at least one primary source per file (listed at the end of each file) and form your own opinion.

** Reading order and rationale
- *Structure first (01, 02, 03, 04)*: the styles available, where boundaries go, how components depend on each other, and how interfaces keep them independent.
- *Patterns and qualities (05, 06)*: reusable solutions with their costs, and the quality attributes and trade-offs that should drive every choice.
- *Decisions and change (07, 08, 09)*: recording decisions, evolving the system safely, and managing the debt that accumulates.
- *Evaluation (10)*: reviewing designs before reality does.
Experienced readers can start from the quality attributes (06) and decision records (07) and return to the structural files as needed. Each file begins with a Staff-level lens that says what the topic means for that role.

** A checklist of Staff-level architectural judgment
Rate yourself honestly; the items are skills, not facts.
- [ ] I derive architecture from forces and evidence, and I can say which forces I am ignoring and why.
- [ ] I choose boundaries by capability, volatility, ownership, and data, and I choose their strength deliberately.
- [ ] I can name the kind of coupling in a design and weigh it by strength, distance, and volatility.
- [ ] I design interfaces as contracts with semantics, failure behavior, and a deprecation policy, and I evolve them compatibly.
- [ ] I can tell when a pattern earns its complexity, and I write down when not to use it.
- [ ] I turn quality requirements into measurable scenarios, quantify trade-offs, and make risk owners explicit.
- [ ] I record significant decisions with alternatives and consequences, with revisit triggers.
- [ ] I plan migrations in reversible slices with measured progress and a finish line, and I guard the architecture with automated checks.
- [ ] I treat debt as an economic decision, and I can make the case to product and business in their language.
- [ ] I run reviews that find real risks quickly, scale through tiers and guardrails, and close the loop.

** Provenance and limits
- The files synthesize the standard literature on software architecture and public documentation from the sources above. They are written at the level of an informed practitioner's notes, and where research or practice is contested (microservices sizing, event sourcing trade-offs, the value of fitness functions in particular contexts), the notes say so.
- The links in this folder were collected from established, well-known locations and were not re-verified one by one when the files were written; confirm a link before relying on it, and prefer the primary source over the summary.
- Code snippets (TypeScript, Java, SQL, protobuf, configuration) are illustrative and were not compiled or run. Numerical examples (availability, capacity, latency) are worked models with stated assumptions, not measurements.
- Architecture is contextual. Treat the heuristics as hypotheses to test against your constraints; when a note contradicts your evidence, trust the evidence and fix the note.
